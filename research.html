Research

From environmental contamination to exposure, dose, and risk.

My research connects mechanistic contaminant fate, evidence synthesis, geospatial analysis, and public-health risk assessment. The common thread is accounting for exposures that are difficult to see because data are sparse, chemicals move across media, or supply chains separate emissions from the people ultimately exposed.

01 / Current PhD research

Globalized and trade-decoupled chemical exposure

International trade can move food, feed, products, waste, and embedded chemicals across long distances. This means the geography of emissions can become separated from the geography of exposure. My PhD develops a forward, mass-conserving source-to-dose framework that follows chemicals from release, through environmental fate and supply chains, into diet and human body burden.

The proof-of-concept focuses on persistent organic pollutants, especially PBDE flame retardants, in the aquaculture supply chain: Peruvian anchoveta to fishmeal and fish oil, fishmeal and fish oil to farmed salmon, and farmed salmon to consumers. The broader goal is to move environmental accounting from an emission-centric view toward an exposure-centric view.

02 / Research framework

A transparent chain from release to receptor

The website now uses one explicit scientific framework as a visual anchor: chemical release, environmental fate, food systems, trade routing, human exposure, and body burden or risk. This is meant to make the research programme legible to environmental chemists, exposure scientists, public-health researchers, and potential collaborators who may enter from different parts of the chain.

03 / Pesticide pollution and risk

Evidence-based pesticide risk assessment in Ethiopia

During my MSc and subsequent work, I built a structured evidence base on pesticide contamination in Ethiopian surface waters. The work combined evidence mapping, systematic review, meta-analysis, field-concentration databases, fate modelling, and ecological and human-health risk assessment.

This research produced a nationwide assessment of pesticide water pollution, human-health risks, and regulatory implications; a meta-analytic ecological risk assessment for pesticides in Ethiopian surface waters; and a site-specific approach for comparing measured and simulated pesticide concentrations. It is a central example of how environmental-health decisions in data-limited settings can still be made more quantitative, transparent, and defensible.

04 / Contaminants and human health

Organochlorine pesticides, heavy metals, and indoor air pollution

P4A

Organochlorine pesticides in Ethiopian waters

I assessed DDT and related organochlorine pesticides in Ethiopian aquatic systems, with attention to bioaccumulation, bioconcentration, fish consumption, water ingestion, and implications for environmental and human health.

Toxicology Reports · aquatic exposure · DDT

P4B

Heavy metals in drinking water

This work synthesized evidence on heavy metals in Ethiopian drinking-water sources and translated measured concentrations into nationwide and regional public-health risk estimates.

Science of the Total Environment · drinking water · public health

P4C

Indoor air pollution in Ethiopian households

This earlier work examined household exposure to indoor air pollution, linking environmental conditions, exposure settings, and public-health relevance in Ethiopia.

05 / Evidence synthesis and geospatial analysis

Systematic review, meta-analysis, GIS, and spatial decision support

A consistent strand of my work is the conversion of scattered environmental evidence into structured, analyzable datasets. I use systematic review and meta-analysis to organize field measurements and exposure estimates, and GIS to represent spatially structured environmental-health problems.

My earlier GIS work used multicriteria evaluation for solid-waste landfill site selection in Harar City, Ethiopia, and contributed a book chapter on GIS applications in solid-waste management. These projects inform my current interest in spatially explicit exposure modelling and geospatial epidemiology.
