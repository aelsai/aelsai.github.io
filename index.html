About

I am a PhD student in Environmental Science in the Wania Group at the University of Toronto Scarborough. My research examines environmental chemical fate, exposure, and health impacts across the source–receptor continuum. Before joining the Wania Group, I was a lecturer at Haramaya University in Ethiopia, where my work focused on environmental and human-health risk assessment, geospatial analysis, and quantitative evidence synthesis.

My doctoral research brings together two complementary themes: non-targeted analysis of passive air samples and modelling of chemical flows, exposures, and inequalities embedded/embodied in international trade. Our recent review on food trade and chemical exposure is available here.

Current research

Two complementary themes connect molecular measurement with global-scale modelling.

Theme 01

Non-targeted analysis of atmospheric chemicals

The Wania Group has developed, validated, and curated a non-targeted analysis workflow for XAD-resin-based passive samplers targeting semivolatile organic compounds in the atmospheric gas phase. I am extending this workflow to the comparatively understudied atmosphere of South America through the LAPAN monitoring network.

Read more →
Theme 02

Globalized and trade-decoupled chemical exposure

Focusing on organic contaminants, my PhD develops a source-to-dose modelling framework that traces contaminants through product life cycles, environmental fate, food-web transfer, trade routes, near- and far-field exposure pathways, and human body burden.
