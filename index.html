02 / About

Environmental exposure science for chemicals that move across borders, media, and food systems.

I am a PhD student in the Wania Group at the University of Toronto Scarborough. My current work asks how global trade can decouple the location of chemical emissions from the location of human exposure. The aim is to connect emissions, environmental fate, food and feed trade, dietary intake, and body burden in a transparent modelling framework.

Before Toronto, I was a lecturer and researcher at Haramaya University in Ethiopia. My earlier work focused on pesticide pollution, heavy metals in drinking water, organochlorine pesticides, indoor air pollution, geospatial analysis, and quantitative evidence synthesis.

03 / Current focus

Trade-decoupled chemical exposure is the scientific centre of the site.

Current PhD research

Persistent organic pollutants in food and feed supply chains

International trade can separate where chemicals are released, where food and feed are produced, where commodities are consumed, and where exposure occurs. My PhD develops source-to-dose modelling approaches that connect contaminant emissions, multimedia fate, food-web transfer, trade routing, dietary intake, and body burden.

The immediate proof-of-concept focuses on PBDE flame retardants and aquaculture-linked food systems. The broader research goal is to move environmental accounting from emission-centred inventories toward exposure-relevant and receptor-aware interpretations.

05 / Methods

A practical toolkit for exposure and risk science.

My work is intentionally method-combining: systematic review when field evidence is scattered, geospatial analysis when exposure is spatially structured, fate modelling when measurements are incomplete, and risk assessment when evidence needs to inform decisions.
